# v0.3 — Fidelity lock

Read `PROJECT.md`, `CHANGELOG.md` and `CLAUDE.md` in full before starting.

This is the critical path milestone. Everything before it was transcription from a spec, which
is work with a clear right answer. This milestone is statistical validation, where a test
failing by 3% could mean a bug in the immunity window, a bug in the opportunity roll, or a
number in the spec that is simply wrong. The discipline that makes it tractable is in Part 2,
and it is not optional.

Work through Parts 0 to 2 before writing implementation code. Part 1 contains one live bug and
three unresolved ambiguities, all of which change measured outcomes.

## Part 1 — Resolve before implementing

### 1a. Live bug: WARDEN's countdown restarts on a repeat success

`Warden.resolve()` sets `warden.countdown_units = units` with no check for an in-flight
countdown. A second successful opportunity during a countdown therefore restarts it.

This is reachable. At AI 1 the countdown is 15 s and opportunities fire every 3.02 s, so
roughly five opportunities land inside it at p=0.05 each, giving about 0.25 expected restarts
per hop. At AI 9 the countdown is 1.667 s and p=0.45, giving a similar rate. Across five path
hops it is a material distortion of arrival time, and §8.3 measures exactly that.

§3.4 does not say what should happen, so decide and record it as a §10 row. **Recommended: a
success while a countdown is in flight is ignored.** The entity is already committed to
moving; a mechanic where repeated successes indefinitely delay a move is the opposite of what
an AI level is supposed to mean, and it would make higher levels non-monotonically slower in a
regime where §8.3 asserts monotonic improvement.

Fix it before deriving §8.3's targets, or you will derive against one model and measure
another.

### 1b. §3.11's roll schedule is ambiguous, and it moves the answer by 40%

§3.11 says each blackout phase rolls "every 5.0 s, 20% chance to advance, guaranteed to advance
at 20.0 s". It does not say whether the first roll happens at t=0 or t=5 s of the phase.

The two readings are not close. Deriving the §8.7 survival rate under each:

| Reading | P(survive 35 s of blackout) |
|---|---|
| First roll at t=5 s | **0.6148** |
| First roll at t=0 s | 0.4389 |

Resolve it explicitly. **Recommended: first roll at t=5 s**, so a phase always takes at least
one interval and the 20 s cap is a genuine ceiling rather than one of five equiprobable
branches. Under that reading the phase completion mass is `{5: 0.2, 10: 0.16, 15: 0.128,
20: 0.512}`, where the mass at 20 s is the guarantee absorbing all three failures.

Record as a §10 row and write the resulting distribution into §3.11 explicitly so it is never
ambiguous again.

### 1c. Two smaller blackout questions

- **Does the 20 s guarantee replace the roll at 20 s or follow it?** Under the recommended
  reading there is no roll at 20 s; the guarantee fires there. State it.
- **Is blackout onset instantaneous within the crossing tick?** Currently power reaches about
  −0.014 before onset is detected, clamped at every exposed surface. That is fine, but §3.11
  should say the phase-1 timer starts from the onset tick, not from the tick power first went
  negative, since with the full sequence these could differ.

## Part 2 — Derive every target before measuring

The v0.2 result is the argument for this. The night-1 derivation gave 0.2397, the measurement
gave 0.2373, and agreement to 0.56 standard errors is strong evidence that both the derivation
and the implementation are right. Neither number alone would have told you anything.

**For every statistical assertion in §8, produce an analytic prediction first, write it into
`CHANGELOG.md`, then measure.** If they agree, both are probably right. If they disagree, you
have a finding, and you found it by having a number to check against rather than a number to
accept.

Which §8 assertions need a derivation:

| Section | Derivable target | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 8.1 | Closed form `(0.1 + 0.1/D) × 535` | Done in v0.1 |
| 8.2 night 1 | 0.2397 | Done in v0.2 |
| 8.2 nights 2–6 | Expected SPRINTER success counts vs threshold 3 | Partly done; complete it |
| 8.3 | Expected STAGE→E_CORNER latency per AI level | **Needed** |
| 8.4 | Attacks per night as a function of peek interval `k` | **Needed** |
| 8.6 | Exact, no sampling | Done |
| 8.7 | 0.6148 under Part 1b's recommended reading | Given below, re-derive it |

### 8.3's derivation shape

Per non-corner hop at AI level `L`: expected opportunities to first success is `20/L`, each
`3.02 s` apart, plus the countdown `ceil_to_tick((1000 − 100L)/60)`. Five hops from `STAGE` to
`E_CORNER`. Assumes 1a is fixed so no restarts occur, and assumes the stage lock is bypassed
and other entities disabled per §8.3, which `entities.*.enabled` now supports.

Predict per level 1 through 10 before measuring. The monotonicity assertion is weak on its own;
agreement with the predicted curve is the real test.

### 8.4's derivation, and a defect in the assertion as written

SPRINTER is frozen while the monitor is up and for a uniform `[0.83, 16.67] s` window sampled
on each monitor-down transition. For a policy peeking 0.5 s every `k` seconds, the unfrozen
window per cycle is `max(0, k − 0.5 − U)`, giving:

| `k` | Unfrozen fraction of the night |
|---|---|
| ≤ 1.33 | **0.00%** |
| 1.5 | 0.06% |
| 2 | 0.71% |
| 4 | 5.63% |
| 6 | 11.47% |
| 8 | 17.55% |
| 10 | 23.73% |
| 15 | 39.32% |
| 20 | 53.75% |
| ∞ | 100% |

**§8.4's flatness assertion is untestable with its own test family.** It asks for "approximately
flat for `k` below the lower bound of the immunity window", but the flat region is
`k ≤ 0.5 + 0.83 = 1.33 s` and the family `{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 15, 20, ∞}` has no point below it.
Between `k=2` and `k=4` the curve rises by a factor of eight, which is the opposite of flat.

Replace it with two assertions that are both stronger and actually testable:

- **A hard zero.** For `k ≤ 1.33 s`, SPRINTER records zero attacks across every seed, because
  the unfrozen window cannot open at all. This is deterministic rather than statistical, and it
  is the sharpest possible test of the immunity mechanic: if a single attack appears, either
  the immunity window or the freeze is wrong. Add `k ∈ {0.75, 1.0, 1.25}` to the family.
- **Agreement with the curve.** Measured attacks per night should track the derived unfrozen
  fraction across the rest of the family, not merely increase monotonically.

Record the replacement in §10 and `CHANGELOG.md` with the derivation attached.

### 8.7's derivation, for cross-checking yours

Replace §8.7's "strictly between 0 and 1" with an agreement test against the derived value at a
stated budget. The original wording is nearly vacuous: it passes for anything except a
completely broken sequence.

## Part 5 — Minimal trace viewer

§9 describes the full v1.2 viewer. Build the subset that helps debug fidelity now, and leave
the rest.

**In scope:** static HTML consuming a JSONL trace, no server and no build step. Twelve-node map
in §9.1's lane geometry with entity tokens. Office panel with both doors (`JAMMED` distinct
from `open`), lights, monitor state and selected camera **by node name**. Power bar, clock,
step counter. Transport controls and a scrubber. Event markers on the scrubber for every
non-null `event`. Termination cause displayed prominently.

**Out of scope until v1.2:** the belief-versus-truth overlay, the policy probability panel and
the `V(s)` sparkline. All three need `env/`, which does not exist. Traces currently carry
`belief` and `policy` as null; the viewer must handle that gracefully rather than erroring, and
must handle a `--stride` subsampled trace without breaking the timeline.

The blackout sequence needs a visible state: which of the three phases is active. It is the one
mechanic where reading JSONL is genuinely painful and the viewer earns its place immediately.

## Working discipline

Use plan mode before Parts 3 and 4. Part 4 in particular is where a wrong turn is expensive,
because a validation suite that encodes the implementation's behaviour rather than the spec's
is worse than no validation suite: it is confidently green and permanently misleading.

Commit at each green run. Tag `v0.3` only when all seven exit criteria pass.

If a measurement disagrees with its derivation, **stop and report it**. Do not adjust the
derivation, do not widen the tolerance, and do not adopt the measured value. One of the two is
wrong and finding out which is the entire point of this milestone.
