# v1.1 — Baseline policy

Read `PROJECT.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `docs/V1.0-DISCREPANCY-RESOLUTION.md` and `CLAUDE.md` before
starting.

v1.0 is sound. `check_env` passes clean, the observation is `Box(0,1,(100,),float32)`, and an
independent leak probe found zero leaks across 33 hidden-state mutations with the monitor down and
lights off, with the non-vacuity converse firing in all four positive cases.

This milestone changes character. Up to here the project has been a software problem with
verifiable right answers. From here it is empirical machine learning: runs take real time, "the
curve is flat" has a dozen causes, and progress comes from experiments rather than from writing
code. Expect it to be slower than v1.0 and to produce fewer clean answers.

All survival figures in this document are **seed-deterministic and will reproduce exactly**. Unlike
v1.0's throughput number, they are not machine-dependent. If any of them differs on your run,
something has changed in the simulator and that is a finding.

## Part 0 — Carry-over from the v1.0 review

Land as `v1.0 follow-ups` before starting training work.

**A1. The environment is not registered with Gymnasium.**

```
gym.make("NightGuard-v0")  ->  NameNotFound: Environment `NightGuard` doesn't exist
```

There is no `register()` call anywhere in the package. §2.4 declares the ID as `NightGuard-v0` and
states that v2.0 adds "additional registered IDs", which presupposes registration already exists.
Criterion 1 was satisfied by constructing `NightGuardEnv` directly, which is legitimate and passes
clean, but the declared ID does not work.

This blocks v1.1 immediately: `sb3`'s `make_vec_env`, most training tooling, and every
config-driven workflow take string IDs. Register `NightGuard-v0` with `night` as a kwarg, plus
whatever curriculum IDs Part 1 settles on, and add a test that `gym.make` returns a working env for
each registered ID.

**A2. §10's curriculum row is stale and contradicts the measurements.**

It reads *"Curriculum start | Night 3 | Night 1 is near-degenerate."* The headroom finding is
recorded in `CHANGELOG.md` and the v0.3 discrepancy doc but never reached §10, which is where a
fresh session looks. Replace it per Part 1 and record the measured series alongside it.

**A3. There is no loadable custom night.** `configs/nights/custom_template.yaml` documents the full
schema; it is not a preset. Part 1 needs one.

## Part 1 — The curriculum, and what the measurements actually say

Measured, 400 seeds per night, `rhythm` frozen at `peek_every_steps=12`:

| Night | `rhythm` | `do_nothing` | `monitor_down` | Headroom above `rhythm` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.000 | 0.287 | 1.000 | 0.000 |
| 2 | 1.000 | 0.010 | 0.990 | 0.000 |
| 3 | **1.000** | 0.000 | 0.840 | **0.000** |
| 4 | 0.993 | 0.000 | 0.003 | 0.007 |
| 5 | 0.973 | 0.000 | 0.007 | 0.027 |
| 6 | 0.325 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.675 |

Night 3 measures 1.000, not the 0.998 recorded earlier. The locked curriculum start is a night
where a fixed script never dies. Nights 4 and 5 leave 0.7% and 2.7%. **Night 6 is the only night
in the shipped set with meaningful room above the reference.**

### Difficulty saturates, and not for the reason you would expect

Raising AI levels above night 6 does almost nothing. Measured, 200 seeds, night-6 escalation:

| Starting levels (W/D/P/S) | `rhythm` |
|---|---|
| 4/10/12/16 (night 6) | 0.345 |
| 4/13/14/18 | 0.235 |
| 6/14/15/19 | 0.215 |
| 8/15/16/20 | 0.185 |
| 10/16/17/20 | 0.185 |
| 14/18/18/20 | 0.185 |
| **20/20/20/20** | **0.185** |

A hard floor at 0.185, identical across four very different configurations. The cause distribution
explains it:

```
(4,13,14,18): KILLED_BLACKOUT 153, SURVIVED 47, entity deaths 0
(20,20,20,20): KILLED_BLACKOUT 163, SURVIVED 37, entity deaths 0
```

`rhythm` never dies to an entity at high difficulty. It closes both doors before every peek, so
nothing ever gets in, and its only failure mode is running out of power under SPRINTER's escalating
bang drain. Once SPRINTER saturates at 20, raising the other three changes nothing.

Three consequences:

1. **`rhythm` is a bad difficulty probe above night 6.** Its score measures power economics, not
   difficulty. Do not calibrate a custom night against it and conclude the night is easy.
2. **This is §3.10's designed tension working**, not a defect. A door-discipline policy is
   power-bound. Record it as a finding rather than treating it as a bug.
3. **It is the most interesting regime for a learned policy.** `rhythm`'s fixed cadence provably
   wastes power: it peeks on a timer regardless of what it knows. An agent that peeks adaptively
   should beat 0.185 substantially. That gap is the headline result this milestone is trying to
   produce.

### Curriculum

Replace §10's row with three stages:

| Stage | Config | `rhythm` reference | Graduation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Night 5 | 0.973 | Mean survival ≥ 0.90 over 500 eval episodes |
| 2 | Night 6 | 0.325 | Mean survival ≥ 0.40 |
| 3 | Custom, 20/20/20/20 with night-6 escalation | 0.185 | Report; no threshold |

Ship stage 3 as `configs/nights/custom_max.yaml` and register it. Nights 1 to 4 are not curriculum
stages: a random policy has room to improve on them, but they saturate at 1.000, so an agent that
graduates learns nothing further. Keep them as sanity checks only.

**One caveat that determines whether stage 1 works at all.** On night 5, `do_nothing` scores 0.000
and `monitor_down` 0.007, so a random policy essentially never reaches dawn. Learning depends
entirely on §6.3's dense survival term rewarding partial nights. **In `sparse_mode` stage 1 will
produce no gradient at all.** Train dense; run sparse only as a final ablation, and expect it to
fail. Say so in the report rather than treating it as a bug.

## Part 2 — Training

`RecurrentPPO` from `sb3-contrib` with an LSTM. Frame stacking is not viable: covering a 15 s
WARDEN countdown needs 30 frames at 0.5 s.

Start with a single configuration and get one curve going up before touching hyperparameters.
The most common failure here is sweeping before the pipeline is known to work.

Record for every run: git SHA, config hash, seed, wall clock, and the full `CHANGELOG.md` entry
pattern. Runs that are not reproducible are not results.

**Revisit vectorisation only after profiling.** §5 of the v1.0 discrepancy doc left `env/vector.py`
unbuilt by decision. Measure the split between environment time and network time on a real run
first. If the environment is genuinely the bottleneck, build it, and the equivalence test against
the object simulator is a hard gate: a vectorised sim that diverges silently invalidates all of §8.

## Part 3 — The measurements that matter

Three results, in descending order of importance. All three go in `CHANGELOG.md`.

**1. The Oracle gap.** Train matched runs with and without the `Oracle` wrapper, same
architecture, same steps, same seeds. The gap is the number that says partial observability is real
rather than assumed.

Note the caveat §6.4 already records: the Oracle exposes true nodes and SPRINTER's stage but not
`immune_until_tick` or the pending attack tick, both of which are hidden and both of which determine
whether a peek is safe. **The measured gap is a lower bound on the true gap.** Report it as one.

**2. Beating `rhythm` on night 6 and custom.** Matching `rhythm` on nights 1 to 5 is not a result;
a fixed script gets 0.973 there. The claim worth making is that a learned policy beats 0.325 and
0.185, and it should beat them by exploiting the thing `rhythm` cannot: adaptive peek timing under
power pressure.

**3. Camera duty cycle falling over training.** The behavioural signature that the agent has learned
to lean on §3.9's audio channel rather than looking constantly. `rhythm` sits at 1/12 ≈ 0.083. An
agent that beats it while peeking *less* is the qualitative result; one that beats it by peeking
more has found a different and less interesting strategy. Log it every evaluation.

## Part 4 — Exit criteria

1. `gym.make` works for every ID declared in §2.4, with a test.
2. A learning curve that rises on stage 1, from a single configuration, before any sweeping.
3. Trained policy beats `do_nothing` on nights 4, 5 and 6 by a margin outside sampling error at
   500 eval episodes.
4. **Trained policy beats `rhythm` on night 6.** This is the milestone's substantive claim.
5. Oracle gap measured on matched runs, non-zero, reported as a lower bound. **If it is zero,
   stop**: either the observation is leaking despite v1.0's probe, or the task does not require
   memory, and both need explaining before proceeding.
6. Camera duty cycle logged across training, with the trend reported either way.
7. Every run reproducible from recorded SHA, config hash and seed.
8. All four §11 gates green, and §6, §7 and §10 describe what the code does.

## Working discipline

Expect this milestone to be slower and messier than every previous one. The exit criteria are
deliberately about *measurements being made and reported*, not about hitting particular numbers,
because the numbers are not knowable in advance and a criterion that demands one invites tuning
until it appears.

If a result disagrees with an expectation, report it. Do not adjust the expectation. The three
places this project has produced real findings — the shadowed validation function, the vacuous
determinism probe, the unreachable §8.4 test family — all came from someone noticing that a check
could not fail and saying so.
