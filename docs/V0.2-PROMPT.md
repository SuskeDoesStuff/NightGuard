### B. Code fixes

**B1. `blackout.enabled: false` is undefined behaviour, and it is in the power model.** With
the flag off, power goes negative and nothing happens. This is the one subsystem everything
else is validated against, so it must not have an undefined corner. Either delete the flag
(no shipped config sets it) or clamp power at 0 and make the guard's semantics explicit in
§3.13. Deleting is preferred.

**B2. `requires-python = ">=3.11"` is an unverifiable support claim.** Type-checking at 3.11
is unverified because numpy's stubs need 3.12+. Raise the floor to `>=3.12` and remove the
problem. Nothing in this project needs 3.11. If you want to keep 3.11, it needs a CI job that
actually runs there; "unverified" is not a third option.

**B3. Split the `Action` concept rather than deferring it.** §1.3 forbids action decoding in
`core/`, and your reasoning that core must accept actions somehow is right, but the fix is a
split, not a deferral:

- `core/` gets a semantic `Action` enum that knows nothing about integers.
- `env/actions.py` owns the `Discrete(17)` index mapping, per §3.2's table.

Doing this now is a rename. Doing it at v1.0 means untangling it from a working simulator and
a trace format that has already serialised the integers.

**B4. Delete dead artifacts.** Remove `configs/presets/v0_smoke.yaml` or wire it into
`validate.py`. Remove `Clock.is_hour_boundary` or use it in the escalation path. Dead config
rots silently and then misleads.

**B5. `timing.time_resolution_s` cannot represent WARDEN's countdown. Change it to `1/300`.**

This is a v0.2 blocker, not a cleanup item. Your exact-integer scheduling machinery works in
v0.1 because the opportunity intervals are two-decimal values. WARDEN's countdown is
`(1000 − 100×ai)/60`, and six of the ten AI levels are non-terminating at millisecond
resolution:

| AI level | Countdown (s) | Exact at 1 ms? |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 4, 7, ≥10 | 15.0, 10.0, 5.0, 0.0 | yes |
| 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 | 13.333…, 11.666…, 8.333…, 6.666…, 3.333…, 1.666… | **no** |

At `time_resolution_s = 0.001` these fail the `conversion_tolerance = 1e-9` check, forcing you
either to round (reintroducing exactly the drift the machinery exists to prevent) or to
special-case one entity's tolerance.

Checked against every timing constant in §3 and §4:

| Resolution | Non-integer constants |
|---|---|
| 1/1000 s (current) | 6 (all non-terminating WARDEN countdowns) |
| 1/100 s | 6 (same) |
| 1/60 s | 6 (all four opportunity intervals, both immunity bounds) |
| **1/300 s** | **0** |

At 1/300 everything is exact: intervals at 906 / 1491 / 1494 / 1503 units, sim tick at 30,
decision step at 150, SPRINTER's immunity bounds at 249 and 5001, and the awkward countdowns
at clean multiples of 500 (AI 2 is 4000 units, AI 9 is 500).

This is not a coincidence. `300 = lcm(60, 100)`, where the 60 comes from the countdown
derivation and the 100 from the two-decimal intervals. Every timing quantity in the spec is
drawn from one of those two families, so 1/300 is the coarsest resolution representing all of
them exactly.

**This does not violate §10's frame-rate lock.** 1/300 s is a resolution, not a frame rate,
and nothing in the codebase should call it one. If anything it strengthens the lock: the `/60`
now disappears into an integer multiplication instead of surviving as a float division.

Add a §4 comment recording the reasoning, and add this test:

> Every timing constant in the loaded config must convert to an exact integer count of
> `time_resolution_s` units within `conversion_tolerance`. Parametrise over all shipped night
> configs.

That test is the durable part. The 1/300 value is a one-off fix; the assertion catches the
next constant that doesn't fit the grid, which would otherwise surface as an inexplicable
off-by-one-tick fidelity failure in v0.3.

## Part 1 — Resolve this before implementing WARDEN

§3.4 contains an ambiguity that makes a documented mechanic unreachable, and it must be
settled before you write the entity.

§3.4's path ends `E_CORNER → OFFICE`, and it specifies a 25%-per-second office kill that
applies while the monitor is **down**. But it also says WARDEN "attacks" when an opportunity
succeeds while `monitor_up` is **true** and the selected camera is not `E_CORNER`.

Read literally, "attacks" means an immediate kill, which means `OFFICE` is never reached and
the entire 25%/s office mechanic is dead code.

**Resolve it as follows, and record it as a §10 row:**

`E_CORNER → OFFICE` is a *move*, not a kill, and it is gated on `monitor_up` exactly as
§3.5's door entities now are. Once in `OFFICE`, the 25%/s kill applies while the monitor is
down, per §3.4 as written. A closed `door_right` still sends WARDEN back to `E_HALL`.

This makes both mechanics live, and it makes the design coherent in a way that matters:

> All three path entities (`WARDEN`, `DRIFTER`, `PROWLER`) can only enter the office while
> the monitor is up. `SPRINTER` is the only entity that punishes keeping the monitor down.

That is precisely the asymmetry §3.5's v0.1 rationale already claims the design has. Confirm
it against CeriW before committing to it, and if CeriW contradicts it, stop and flag rather
than implementing either reading.

**Consequence you need to hold in mind for Part 2:** under this resolution, a policy that
never raises the monitor is immune to `WARDEN`, `DRIFTER` and `PROWLER`. `SPRINTER` alone
carries the entire difficulty gradient for `do_nothing`.

## Part 2 — Derive before you implement

Before writing `SPRINTER`, derive its expected night-1 `do_nothing` survival rate
analytically from §3.7 and §3.3. Write the derivation into `CHANGELOG.md` as part of the v0.2
entry.

Inputs: opportunity interval 5.01 s, night length 535 s, night-1 `SPRINTER` levels 0 at
start with +1 at 3AM and +1 at 4AM, three successes to arm, forced attack 25 s after arming,
open left door. `do_nothing` never raises the monitor, so no camera freeze and no immunity
window apply, and never closes a door, so no bangs and no stage resets.

**Do this before reading the next paragraph, then compare.**

My own derivation gives roughly 18 opportunities at level 1 and 35 at level 2, an expected
4.4 successes against a threshold of 3, and a survival probability near 0.19. If your figure
differs materially, one of us has misread §3.7 and that is worth resolving before any code
exists.

### The §8.2 collision this creates

§8.2 currently requires night-1 `do_nothing` survival ≥ 0.8 and calls it the single most
diagnostic assertion in the suite. That was my number and it is wrong. It cannot be met by a
faithful `SPRINTER`.

Worse, the whole table collapses. Running the same derivation for nights 2 through 6 gives
expected success counts far above the threshold of 3, so `do_nothing` survival is
indistinguishable from zero from night 2 onward. §8.2's strict monotonic chain (`night 3 <
night 2`, `night 4 < night 3`) cannot be satisfied at 10,000 samples when every value is 0.

**Do not fix this by adjusting the threshold after measuring.** Restructure §8.2 instead:

- **Night 1:** assert that measured survival agrees with the analytic derivation within
  binomial sampling error. This is a far stronger test than a threshold, and it restores the
  "most diagnostic assertion" role properly, because it can fail in both directions.
- **Nights 2 to 6:** assert survival is non-increasing, permitting ties, and bounded above by
  a small constant. Record measured values per §8.0.
- **Keep:** `rhythm` must beat `do_nothing` on nights 3 through 6.
- **Add a note** explaining that near-zero `do_nothing` survival from night 2 is expected and
  correct, because `SPRINTER` is the sole kill path for a monitor-down policy.

Record the restructure in §10 and `CHANGELOG.md` with the derivation attached.

## Part 3 — Implementation scope

**WARDEN** (§3.4): fixed path including `E_HALL`, stage lock behind `DRIFTER` and `PROWLER`,
universal camera suppression of opportunity rolls, the `(1000 − 100×ai)/60` second countdown
that is **not** paused by monitor raises, inverted rules at `E_CORNER`, office entry per
Part 1, 25%/s office kill while the monitor is down, and no forced monitor-down (holding the
monitor up indefinitely must remain a legal if expensive strategy).

The countdown must be held on the exact integer grid per B5, not rounded to the sim tick. It
is decremented every tick and is **not** paused by monitor raises: only new opportunity rolls
are suppressed by the monitor, never an in-flight countdown. Exit criterion 3 tests precisely
this, so write that test before the implementation rather than after.

**SPRINTER** (§3.7): stage counter with no position, universal camera freeze, the
uniform `[0.83, 16.67]` s post-monitor-down immunity sampled once per transition, arming at
3 successes, attack on next monitor raise or 25 s whichever is first, 0.5 s grace period,
door bang with `1.0 + 5.0×n` drain, stage reset to uniform `{0, 1}` after a bang.

**Trace format** (§5): header, one record per sim tick, footer. Ground truth in the trace even
though the agent never sees it. `--stride N` on the writer.

The `belief` and `policy` blocks in §5 are produced by `env/`, which does not exist yet. Emit
`null` for both in v0.2 and keep the keys present so the shape is stable, per §5's "never
changes shape afterwards".

## Part 4 — Exit criteria

§7's v0.2 criteria stand, amended:

1. All four entities can produce a kill in simulation, verified by seeded tests.
2. Same seed plus same action script produces a byte-identical trace file. **The script must
   satisfy §8.0:** assert at least 10 distinct trace hashes across 40 seeds before asserting
   within-seed reproducibility. An all-`NOOP` script is vacuous here for the same reason it
   was vacuous in v0.1.
3. `WARDEN`'s countdown is not paused by monitor raises, verified by a targeted test.
4. `SPRINTER` cannot advance while the monitor is up, and cannot advance during the immunity
   window after it comes down.
5. `WARDEN` cannot leave `STAGE` while either door entity is on `STAGE`.

**New, added because of Part 1:**

6. Measured night-1 `do_nothing` survival agrees with the Part 2 derivation within binomial
   sampling error at 10,000 episodes.
7. **No degenerate monitor-down exploit.** Construct the best monitor-down policy you can
   (doors managed by audio cues alone, monitor never raised) and measure it against `rhythm`
   on nights 5 and 6. If it wins, the environment has a degenerate strategy and Part 1's
   resolution needs revisiting. Report the numbers either way; this is a finding, not a
   pass/fail gate on your implementation.

All four §11 gates must be green: pytest, ruff, mypy --strict, validate.py.
