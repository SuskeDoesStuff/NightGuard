## Part 0 — Carry-over from the v0.3 review

Land as `v0.3 follow-ups` before starting v1.0 work.

**A1. Power runs far below zero and nothing clamps it. This is a v1.0 blocker.**

`sim.py` line 265 triggers blackout at `power_pct <= 0.0` but never clamps. Because SPRINTER's
door bang applies a discrete cost of `1 + 5n` percentage points, a late bang can overshoot zero
by a large margin in a single tick, and the idle drain then continues throughout the blackout.

Measured on night 6, both doors held, 300 seeds:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Power at blackout onset (seed 0) | **−5.18 pp** |
| Power at death (seed 0) | −11.58 pp |
| Most negative across 300 seeds | **−19.47 pp** |
| Drain rate during blackout | 0.1333 pp/s, unchanged |

There is no current symptom because every exposed surface clamps: `result()` and both
serialisation points in `trace/schema.py`. That is exactly why it is dangerous now. §6.2
declares the observation `Box(low=0.0, high=1.0)` and encodes power as `power / 99.0`, which
would emit −0.20 and violate the declared space. Depending on the checker that is either a hard
failure in criterion 1 or, worse, a silent out-of-range value the policy learns against.

Fix both halves:

- Clamp to `0.0` at blackout onset, in the same branch that sets the blackout flag.
- Skip the drain step entirely while `state.blackout` is true. Power is exhausted; continuing
  to model it as a shrinking negative quantity is meaningless.

Then remove the now-redundant `max(0.0, ...)` guards at the three exposed surfaces, or keep them
and add a test asserting `power_pct >= 0.0` at every tick of every episode. Prefer the test:
it fails loudly if a future discrete cost reintroduces the problem.

**A2. `--force-blackout-at` does not zero power, and the trace shows it.**

The sample trace from `--night 1 --seed 2 --no-entities --force-blackout-at 500` terminates with
`cause: KILLED_BLACKOUT` and `final_power: 23.789583`, and power visibly drains from 26.98 to
23.79 across the blackout in the viewer.

§8.7's wording is "power forced to 0 at t=500 s". The harness forces the blackout *state*
without forcing the *power*, so the trace depicts a blackout with a quarter of the battery
remaining. §8.7's measurement is unaffected, since the phase sequence does not read power, but
the artifact is internally contradictory and it is the one trace a human will actually look at.

Set `power_pct = 0.0` when forcing. With A1 applied, the drain then stops and the trace reads
0.0 throughout the blackout, which is what §3.11 describes.

**A3. Confirm what "onset tick" means.** In the sample trace the `blackout` event is logged at
tick 5001, but the phase arithmetic runs from tick 5000: SONG at 5150 is 150 ticks later (the
third 5 s roll), KILL at 5250 is 100 ticks after that (the second roll), death at 5290 is 40
ticks later (the second 2 s roll). All three are correct, so the implementation is anchored at
5000 while the event is stamped at 5001. §10's 1c row resolved that "phase 1 starts from the
onset tick"; make the two ticks agree or state explicitly which one is the onset.

**A4. Fast-suite runtime is trending back toward the problem A5 solved.** It is 22 s on your
machine and 76 s here, up from 6.4 s at the v0.2 follow-ups. §6 adds observation and reward
tests to every existing fixture. Audit what is actually fast before adding more: several §8
characterisation tests are probably slow tests wearing a fast marker.

**A5. Close out v0.3's criterion 5 properly, and extend §8.0 to cover it.**

The viewer was verified statically because there is no browser in the agent environment, and the
suggested fixture was `--night 1 --seed 2 --no-entities --force-blackout-at 500`. That trace
cannot exercise most of what criterion 5 asks for. With the roster disabled, all four entity
tokens sit on `STAGE` for all 5,290 records, no entity event ever fires, and `jams` is
`(False, False)` in every record, so the `JAMMED` versus `open` distinction the check demands is
not merely unverified but unreachable.

This is E2's failure mode one level down: a verification step that reports nothing while
appearing to report success. Use two fixtures instead, both confirmed to exercise what they
claim:

```bash
# 1131 records: jams (False,False)->(False,True), PROWLER 0,1,7,8,9,10,11 into the OFFICE,
# DRIFTER 0->1->6, invasion_prowler and death_prowler, footstep and kitchen audio,
# cause KILLED_PROWLER
python scripts/export_trace.py --night 5 --seed 0 --policy random --out trace.jsonl

# 948 records: WARDEN's full path 0,1,7,8,9,10, SPRINTER stages 0->3,
# warden_countdown_start and warden_retreat, natural blackout, death_blackout
python scripts/export_trace.py --night 6 --seed 7 --policy rhythm --stride 5 --out trace.jsonl
```

The second reaches blackout without forcing, which makes it a better viewer fixture than the
forced one. Keep the forced trace as §8.7's isolated fixture only.

Then add to §8.0:

> This applies to human verification steps as well as automated assertions. A manual check
> requires a fixture capable of exercising it, and that fixture must be named alongside the
> check rather than assumed. A verification step that cannot fail reports nothing.

## Part 1 — Decide before writing code: the vectorisation criterion

§7's v1.0 exit criterion 4 requires **≥ 50,000 decision steps per second per core at N=256**.

Measured current throughput on night 4, single environment, all-`NOOP`:

```
13,031 decision steps/s   (85.9 episodes/s)
shortfall against the criterion: 3.8x
```

The gap is not a tuning problem. The core is a Python object graph with per-entity method
dispatch, dataclass attribute access, and a `Generator` call per entity per firing. Vectorising
N=256 in lockstep amortises interpreter overhead across environments and would plausibly clear
50k, but only by rewriting the transition function as array operations over a struct-of-arrays
state. That is a second implementation of every mechanic in §3.

**That is the decision, and it is an architectural one.** Three options:

1. **Write the vectorised sim, keep the object sim as the reference, and add an equivalence
   test.** Both implementations run the same seeds and action scripts and must produce identical
   per-tick state signatures. This is the only option that keeps v0.3's validation work
   meaningful, because §8 is written against the object sim. Cost: a second implementation, plus
   an equivalence harness that must cover blackout, jams, invasions and all four entities.
2. **Drop the criterion to what a single-env implementation plus subprocess vectorisation
   achieves**, roughly 13k × cores. Cost: PPO wall-clock rises proportionally, which matters most
   in v2.0 when you are sweeping randomised configs.
3. **Defer the criterion to v1.1**, where the training loop will tell you empirically whether
   throughput is actually the bottleneck.

**Recommend option 3, and here is why the criterion should not drive this decision.**

The 50,000 figure was never derived from anything. §3's feasibility argument states the actual
requirement: throughput that "puts a full PPO run in the tens of minutes on a laptop". Check that
against measured performance rather than against the number:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| v1.1 baseline run | 2,000,000 decision steps |
| Env time at 13,031 steps/s | **154 s** |
| Env time with observation encoding, assume 8,000 steps/s | ~250 s |
| Typical RecurrentPPO wall clock for 2M steps with an LSTM on CPU | tens of minutes |

The environment is already a single-digit percentage of v1.1's wall clock. Vectorising to 50k
would save perhaps three minutes out of thirty, and the LSTM forward and backward passes dominate
regardless. **The criterion's own purpose is already met at current throughput.**

Option 1's cost, meanwhile, is not a one-off. It is two implementations of every mechanic
maintained in lockstep through v2.0's randomisation, timing jitter and rare-noise adversary, each
of which must then be added twice and covered by the equivalence harness. That is a permanent tax
paid for a saving that does not currently bind.

So: **amend §7's criterion 4 to state the requirement in terms of its purpose** rather than a
throughput number, record a §10 row, and revisit in v1.1 with the training loop as the benchmark.
If v1.1 profiling shows the env is genuinely the bottleneck, vectorise then, against a measured
need and with the equivalence test as a hard gate.

Replace criterion 4 with:

> A 100,000-step rollout using the v1.1 policy architecture completes in under 60 seconds of
> environment time on one core, measured with observation encoding active. Record the figure.

If you disagree after profiling, say so with numbers rather than proceeding on either reading.

Whichever is chosen, the equivalence test is the load-bearing part. A vectorised sim that
diverges from the validated one silently invalidates all of §8.

## Part 2 — Observation encoding

§6.2's 98 dimensions, exactly as specified. The design rule is the one that matters: **give the
agent exactly what a human player perceives, and nothing more.**

| Block | Dims | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Resources | 2 | `power / 99.0` (requires A1), `time_s / 535.0` |
| Office | 5 | doors, lights, monitor |
| Camera | 12 | 11 cameras one-hot plus a "monitor down" slot |
| Belief × 4 | 56 | 14 per entity: 12-dim last-observed node, `ticks_since_observed`, `visible_now` |
| Audio | 4 | per §3.9, from `step_audio`, not the per-tick channel |
| Door proximity | 2 | non-zero **only** on a step in which that light was flashed |
| Last action | 17 | one-hot |

Three details that are easy to get wrong:

**SPRINTER's belief block is irregular.** It has no node, so its 12-dim slot encodes stage as a
one-hot over `{unknown, 0, 1, 2, 3}` in the first five positions with the rest zero. Document it
at the point of encoding; it is the one asymmetry in an otherwise uniform layout.

**`ticks_since_observed` normalises as `min(ticks, 600) / 600`.** That saturates at 60 s, which
is roughly twelve DRIFTER moves and well past the point where belief carries information. If you
disagree with the saturation point after implementing, say so with a reason rather than changing
it silently.

**An entity is observed if and only if** the monitor is up, the entity's node equals the selected
camera, and that node has a video feed; or the entity is at a door corner and the corresponding
light was flashed this step. `E_KITCHEN` has no video, so selecting it never yields a visual
observation. Its occupancy reaches the agent only through the `kitchen` audio signal.

**Jams need a decision.** §6.2's office block has five bits and does not include jam state, but a
jammed door is not the same as an open one and the agent has no way to learn the difference from
a door-toggle that silently no-ops. Either add two dims and amend §6.2, or state explicitly that
jam state is deliberately hidden. Recommend adding them: the human player can see the door is
stuck.

## Part 3 — Reward

§6.3 exactly:

```
+0.01   per decision step survived
+10.0   reaching 6AM
-10.0   any death
-0.002 × (power drained this step − idle drain this step)
-0.05   on entering blackout, once
```

The power term reads drain, so it depends on A1. With the drain skipped during blackout the term
is naturally zero there, which is correct: there is nothing left to spend.

Implement `reward.sparse_mode` as specified. It is the harder benchmark and v2.0 reports both.

**Do not add shaping terms.** Not for camera coverage, not for door discipline, not for anything.
The peek-then-react loop emerging unaided is the result; a shaping term that encodes it is the
result being assumed.

## Part 5 — Exit criteria

§7's v1.0 criteria, amended:

1. `gymnasium.utils.env_checker.check_env` passes with no warnings.
2. 10,000 random-policy episodes across all six nights complete without exception.
3. **No position leak.** A test that mutates a hidden entity's node and asserts the observation
   is bit-identical. Run it for all four entities, in and out of the office, jammed and not.
   This is the most important test in the milestone.
4. Throughput per whichever Part 1 option was chosen. Under the recommended option 3, a
   100,000-step rollout with observation encoding active completes in under 60 s of environment
   time on one core, and the figure is recorded. If option 1 was chosen instead, the equivalence
   test between object and vectorised sims passes on 100 seeds × random action scripts with
   identical per-tick state signatures.
5. `reset(seed=k)` twice produces identical trajectories under a fixed action sequence.
6. **`power_pct >= 0.0` at every tick of every episode**, and the observation vector is within
   its declared `Box` bounds at every step of the 10,000-episode run. Assert the bounds directly
   rather than trusting `check_env` to catch it.
7. All four §11 gates green, and §6 in `PROJECT.md` describes what the code does.

## Working discipline

Use plan mode for Parts 1 and 2. Part 1 is an architectural commitment and Part 2 is where a
subtle error is invisible: an observation that leaks position produces a policy that learns
beautifully and means nothing, and the learning curve looks better, not worse.

Commit at each green run. Tag `v1.0` when all seven criteria pass.

If a measurement disagrees with a derivation, stop and report. Do not adjust either.
